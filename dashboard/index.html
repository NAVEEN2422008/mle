About the Solar Flare Early Warning System (SFEWS)

What is this project?
The Solar Flare Early Warning System (SFEWS) is an end-to-end deep learning space weather forecasting cockpit engineered for operational deployment at ISRO. It predicts major M- and X-class solar flares 15 to 60 minutes before peak destructive radiation impacts Earth's magnetosphere.

Why is it mission critical?
Extreme solar flares release high-energy X-ray irradiance and driver coronal mass ejections (CMEs) that cause global GNSS satellite navigation failure, HF radio communication blackouts for trans-polar aviation, and geomagnetic geomagnetically induced currents (GICs) that can destroy power grid transformers. Early warning enables proactive satellite re-orientation and grid protective switching.

Orbital Payloads & Datasets:
- ISRO Aditya-L1 (XSM / SoLEXS): Hard X-ray spectrometer in halo orbit around Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 1 (PRADAN Level-1 data).
- NOAA GOES-18 EXIS: High-cadence Soft X-Ray irradiance (0.1–0.8 nm & 0.05–0.4 nm).
- NASA SDO / HMI: High-resolution line-of-sight vector magnetograms.

Deep Learning & Physics Architecture:
A 4-tier neural ensemble comprising a 30-Dimensional Spatio-Temporal Graph Transformer and CNN-LSTM, constrained by Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) thermal conduction equations to guarantee zero violation of plasma physics.
